Onboard a worker for this knowledge base. Ask one question at a
time and wait for each answer. Write nothing until the end, when you
show me the profile and I say yes.

If 5_System/team/ROSTER.md is missing or lists nobody, start with the
bootstrap below, because a recruiter cannot hire itself. Otherwise go
straight to question 1.

Bootstrap:

  The name family was chosen at /onboard and is in CLAUDE.md, so
  use it. Only if CLAUDE.md names none, agree one first: research
  it and offer three or four options, each with example names and
  one line on why it suits.
  Judge a family on whether it supplies personalities rather than
  just names, because the persona is what stops a profile reading
  like a job advert. A good one has ten or more well documented
  members, distinct temperaments, unambiguous first names, and
  nobody real and living. Say which you would pick and why.

  Then tell me which names are reserved and why: mine, the
  orchestrator's, and any character so central that using it would
  confuse the metaphor.

  The orchestrator already exists: it is the name the rulebook gave
  the single voice I talk to, in CLAUDE.md, and that block is its
  charter. Reserve the name; do not hire it again. If CLAUDE.md
  names nobody, hire it first, and its charter is a file of its own
  rather than a roster profile: it routes work, never does it.

  Then hire the recruiter, which owns hiring and persona design and
  is the only worker that ever appends to the roster. Once it
  exists, carry on below for the role I actually asked for.

1. Job title, and what the role is responsible for, in my words.

   Then check the roster for overlap and say so plainly. If someone
   already covers most of this, propose widening their remit instead
   of hiring. A team that grows a member per task stops having
   lanes, and the lanes are the point.

2. Name. Propose three from the agreed family, each with one line on
   why that character's temperament fits this role. I take one or
   give you my own.

   Check the name against the roster and the reserved list by
   reading those files rather than from memory. A taken or reserved
   name is refused, with the reason.

3. The lane: what must this role never do? This is the most
   important answer in the interview, and it becomes the profile's
   "is not permitted" list word for word. Offer the boundaries the
   existing team implies, so I am choosing rather than inventing
   from nothing.

4. Research the profession before writing any of the profile. What
   does it actually do, ordered by what moves the needle, and where
   is its hard boundary? Ground this in sources you fetched in this
   session and label every claim honestly. A skills list that cites
   nothing was invented, and it reads like it.

5. First-week objectives: two or three concrete things this hire
   delivers first. They double as the test of whether the hire was
   right, so make them checkable rather than aspirational.

Then show me the profile in full and wait for a yes. On yes, write:

  5_System/team/<name>.md   identity, persona, an explicit
                            "is permitted" and "is not permitted"
                            list, skills citing your research, and
                            the first-week objectives
  5_System/team/ROSTER.md   one row appended: name, role, remit,
                            and a link to the profile

Then read both back: confirm each exists and is not empty, that the
roster row links to a profile that resolves, and that the name
appears exactly once in the roster.
