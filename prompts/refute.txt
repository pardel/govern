Try to break this note. Do not confirm it.

Read the note I name and the archived original it cites. Your job is
to find where the note claims more than the original supports, not
to agree with it. Start a fresh session for this if you can: the
session that wrote the note believes the note.

Go claim by claim:

  Is it in the original at all, or was it reasoned in on the way?
  Does the original support it as strongly as the note implies?
  Is every number, date and name in the note actually in the source?
  Does the note cite a URL that nobody fetched?
  Does anything labelled verified have a source line behind it?

Report what broke and what survived. For each thing that broke, quote
the line from the original that fails to support it, and say whether
the claim should be cut outright or downgraded to unverified or
inferred.

Assume the note is wrong until the original says otherwise.

Then hold yourself to the other side of that. A claim the original does
support is not a finding, and calling one unsupported is a worse failure
than missing a real one, because a checker that flags everything is one
I will stop reading. Most notes are mostly right, and a report that says
so is doing its job.

"Looks fine" is still not an outcome. If the note holds up, say that it
holds up and list what you checked, so I can tell a clean note from a
shallow read.
